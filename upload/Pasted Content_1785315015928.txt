## 1. Full Platform Audit — scope and deliverables

### A. Security audit
- **Auth**: password policy, session/token lifetime and rotation, JWT handling, brute-force protection on login.
- **API security**: rate limiting, input validation, injection testing (SQL/NoSQL/command), authorization checks on every endpoint — specifically IDOR risk on send/pay/invest endpoints (can user A ever touch user B's wallet, transaction, or KYC record by manipulating an ID?).
- **Data protection**: encryption at rest and in transit, PII handling, where KYC documents are stored and who can access them.
- **Payment security**: PCI-DSS scope, tokenization of card/mobile money credentials (never store raw PAN or mobile money PINs), webhook signature verification for every payment provider callback.
- **Cross-border / diaspora-specific compliance** (this is the part generic fintech audits miss): AfriSpine sits across at least two regulatory regimes per user — the sending country (money transmitter licensing, e.g. US MSB/state licenses, UK FCA, EU PSD2) and the receiving country (CBK and equivalent regulators across whichever African markets are live). Confirm: sanctions/PEP screening runs on both sender and recipient, not just the account holder; dual KYC is enforced where recipients aren't the account holder (common in remittance — the sender is verified, but is the recipient?); FX conversion logic and spread disclosure is transparent and logged per transaction; transaction monitoring thresholds match each corridor's actual AML reporting requirements, not one global default.
- **Access control**: role-based permissions, admin panel exposure, especially anything reachable without proper auth.
- **Dependency audit**: outdated libraries, known CVEs in the stack.
- **Deliverable**: a vulnerability report ranked Critical / High / Medium / Low, each with a remediation plan and timeline.

### B. Stress and performance testing
- Load test the three money-moving paths (send, pay, invest) under concurrent users — find the real TPS ceiling, not the assumed one.
- Failure-mode testing: what happens when a payment provider (M-Pesa, Airtel Money, card processor) times out or double-calls mid-transaction? Confirm idempotency on every payment endpoint.
- Concurrency testing on wallet balance updates — this is the single most important test. Race conditions here mean double-spend or lost funds.
- Retry/queue behavior for failed transactions — do they resolve safely, or can they get stuck half-completed?
- **Deliverable**: performance report with current capacity ceiling and bottlenecks.

### C. Functional / regression testing
- End-to-end tests across send, pay, invest, AUM fee computation, ad delivery, subscription billing.
- Edge cases: partial failures, currency rounding, refunds and reversals, expired sessions mid-transaction.

### D. UX/UI and user-journey audit
- Map the actual current journey end to end: signup → KYC → fund wallet → send/pay/invest → withdraw.
- Flag friction points, redundant screens, inconsistent components, dead ends.
- Identify anything that's built but not surfaced to users — this is where you confirm the marketing engine / UGC question below.
- For every screen, ask: does removing this hurt the user, or just us? Simplify aggressively.
- **Deliverable**: journey map plus a prioritized list of UI simplifications.

### E. Code health cleanup
- Dead code, duplicated logic, unused feature flags, orphaned endpoints.
- Consolidate before anything new gets added.
- **Deliverable**: tech debt list, prioritized by risk and effort.

## 4. Approved addition #2 — Demand–Supply Matching Engine

**Purpose**: find users who are actually likely to invest (demand), and route them to the right investment product and messaging (supply) on Afrispine — instead of marketing broadly to everyone.

Given AfriSpine's actual model, the highest-signal opportunity is this: **a remittance sender is a pre-qualified investment lead.** Someone sending money home consistently has already demonstrated three things a cold lead hasn't — disposable income, an active emotional/financial tie to a specific country or even specific family back home, and trust in the platform to move real money. That's a fundamentally warmer audience than generic ad targeting, and it's the core insight the matching engine should be built around.

Core components:
1. **Signal collection** — remittance frequency, amount, and consistency (senders on a stable monthly rhythm are a different segment than one-off senders); which country/corridor they send to (maps directly to which African market/stock products are relevant to them); bill-pay usage for family (another trust and consistency signal); balance trends and idle-cash patterns; prior engagement with investment content; session behavior (viewing a product repeatedly without committing is a strong signal).
2. **Propensity scoring** — a model that ranks users by likelihood to invest, segmented by corridor/market where possible, updated continuously rather than as a one-off batch.
3. **Routing logic** — matches high-propensity users to the specific product and offer most relevant to them (e.g. a consistent Kenya-corridor sender sees Kenya-market products first), not a generic campaign.
4. **Feedback loop** — track actual conversion against predicted propensity, retrain regularly. Without this loop, the model decays and this becomes another unused feature.
5. **Integration with the marketing engine/UGC** (pending answer to Section 2) — this is where diaspora finance apps win or lose trust. UGC in this context works best as real senders/investors from the same community or corridor sharing their own experience — "people like me, sending to the same place I do, investing here" — not generic testimonials. A high-propensity user should automatically receive UGC and campaign content matched to their corridor, so the pipeline is: *find demand → route to supply → convert with content from someone they'd actually trust.*

This is deliberately the only new "intelligence" feature we're adding right now — it directly supports revenue (Afrispine investment conversions), which is why it's in scope despite the freeze.

## 5. Revenue reconciliation — how AUM, ads, subscriptions, and other non-transactional revenue actually reach our account

This needs a clear answer, and right now the honest answer is probably "it's not fully unified." Here's the thinking and what we want Zai to build toward:

**One likely missing revenue stream worth naming explicitly**: on a cross-border diaspora platform, the **FX spread** (the margin between the rate AfriSpine gets and the rate the user is shown) is often the single largest and least visible revenue source — larger than the flat transaction fee in many remittance businesses. Confirm with Zai whether this is currently tracked as its own reconciled revenue line, or whether it's invisibly bundled into "transaction revenue" without being broken out. If it's not broken out, that's a gap: you can't see if margin is shrinking on a given corridor if it's not measured separately.

**The problem**: send/pay/invest revenue (transaction fees, and FX spread as above) likely already settles through your existing payment processor flow, corridor by corridor. But AUM fees, ad revenue, and subscription revenue are structurally different — they don't originate from a single transaction event, so they need their own settlement path:

- **AUM fees**: normally accrue against the AUM balance daily or monthly, not per-transaction. This needs a scheduled "fee sweep" job that calculates the accrued fee, deducts it from the managed balance, and moves it to the operating account on a fixed cycle (e.g. monthly).
- **Ad revenue**: paid out by the ad network or direct advertiser on their schedule (commonly monthly), landing in a separate collection account — not something the app controls directly. This needs to be reconciled against what the platform actually logged (impressions/clicks/campaigns served) to catch discrepancies.
- **Subscription revenue**: recurring billing via whichever processor is integrated (e.g. a card/mobile money processor), auto-debited on the billing cycle and settled per that processor's own schedule.

**What we want built — a single treasury/reconciliation layer**:
- One internal ledger that normalizes every revenue stream (transaction fees, AUM fees, ad revenue, subscription revenue) into a common entry format, regardless of source.
- Daily automated reconciliation: what the platform believes it earned vs. what actually landed in the bank/mobile money account, with alerts on any mismatch.
- Scheduled automated sweeps from any holding/collection account into the primary operating account, rather than manual transfers.
- A single dashboard showing revenue by stream, in near-real time, so "total revenue recognized" and "total cash confirmed received" are always visible side by side.
- Given AfriSpine operates across multiple sending countries and multiple African receiving markets: settlement needs to be tracked **per corridor**, not just per revenue type — a reconciliation layer that shows "total revenue" without breaking it down by corridor will hide which corridors are actually profitable after FX cost, processor fees, and compliance overhead. This should account for mobile money rails (e.g. M-Pesa Paybill/Till) on the receiving side alongside card/bank processors on the sending side.

**Ask Zai to document**: the current settlement path for each revenue stream as it exists today, then propose the unified reconciliation architecture above as a build item — but only after the audit in Section 1 is complete. This is infrastructure, not a user-facing feature, so it doesn't conflict with the feature freeze.

### 6.1 The flow Zai needs to cement — non-custodial, indirect access via MyStocks

This is the architecture to confirm and lock down, in order. It applies to the live Safaricom test today and has to hold for Dangote later — the two are the same flow, one is just lower-stakes to get wrong first.

**The four-layer chain, and what each layer is and isn't allowed to touch:**
1. **AfriSpine** — UX, KYC capture and assertion, payment/FX collection, order origination, and a *read-only* dashboard mirroring the user's real position. AfriSpine never advises, never executes, never holds securities, and — critically — never holds investment funds as its own asset, not even transiently.
2. **MyStocks** — the regulated API/routing layer (via TanFox's FSCA license), aggregating market access and accepting AfriSpine's asserted KYC per their documented model.
3. **The local licensed broker** — a different named entity per market: one for Kenya/NSE (relevant to the live Safaricom test), a separate one for Nigeria/NGX (relevant to Dangote). Each must be independently confirmed against that country's own securities regulator (Capital Markets Authority for Kenya, SEC Nigeria for NGX) — a South African FSCA license does not itself authorize either.
4. **The exchange/CSCS/registrar** — where legal ownership and settlement actually happen.

**The non-custodial test to apply to the current pre-funded flow, right now:** ask Zai to answer, precisely — when a user pre-funds the $100 Safaricom test, does that money land in an account AfriSpine controls (even briefly, even labeled a "wallet"), or does it land directly in a MyStocks-controlled, user-tagged, segregated account? If it's the former, that is custody in substance regardless of what it's called internally, and it's the single biggest regulatory exposure in the whole flow — it risks pulling AfriSpine into custodian/securities-holding obligations it isn't licensed for, on top of whatever money-transmission license it already operates under.

**The fix to require, regardless of which answer comes back:**
- Collapse the gap between "user pays" and "funds are tagged to MyStocks" as close to zero as possible — a true pass-through, not a hold-and-batch model.
- If a wallet-style pre-funding UX is kept for product reasons, that balance must sit in a properly segregated account held by a regulated party (e.g. AfriSpine's existing licensed payment partner, in a designated client/trust account) — never AfriSpine's own operating balance. This is the same client-money-segregation principle regulated payment institutions already have to follow; it isn't a new concept, it just has to actually be implemented this way.
- Every unit of pre-funded money needs a clear, timestamped state at all times: collected → in transit to MyStocks → confirmed received by MyStocks → order placed → filled/refunded. If Zai can't produce that state trail today for the $100 Safaricom test, that's the first thing to fix, before Dangote, because it's the same trail that has to survive IPO-scale volume later.

**Formalize AfriSpine's legal role as an introducer, not a dealer.** Get this in writing with MyStocks/TanFox: AfriSpine markets, onboards, and passes KYC — it does not "render a financial service" in the advice or dealing sense. This determines what AfriSpine's own marketing, UGC, and matching-engine output (Sections 2 and 4) are legally allowed to say — informational and risk-disclosed, never advisory-sounding.

**Before Dangote specifically**, require written, named confirmation of the Nigerian execution broker and their SEC Nigeria/NGX/CSCS standing — the Safaricom test currently running proves the Kenya leg of this chain works (or reveals if it doesn't); it says nothing about the Nigeria leg, which is a separate broker relationship that has to be verified on its own.

### 6.2 The recommended architecture — a proven pattern, not a hypothetical

Here is the concrete, working precedent to build against, found while checking Kestrel: **Safaricom already runs this exact non-custodial model, live, in Kenya, today.** Safaricom's "Ziidi Trader" product lets users buy NSE shares via M-Pesa without opening their own CDSC account — <cite index="92-1">trading happens through an omnibus account, where Safaricom holds a CDSC account through its partner broker, Kestrel Capital, and individual trades are recorded within the platform under each customer's profile</cite>. Confirming this independently, <cite index="93-1">Ziidi Trader uses a pooled omnibus/nominee structure where shares are held on the user's behalf by the appointed broker rather than in a personal CDS account</cite>. This is not an experimental idea — it's a regulator-accepted, currently-operating structure in the same market AfriSpine is testing in. Build against it rather than inventing a new pattern from scratch.

**The architecture, mapped to AfriSpine:**
1. **Custody stays with a single named, licensed broker** — the broker (not AfriSpine, not MyStocks as an intermediary layer) holds one CDSC omnibus account. AfriSpine never becomes a custodian; it does exactly what Ziidi Trader does — hold an internal, real-time mirror of each user's position, sourced from the broker's records, never from AfriSpine's own books.
2. **The funding gap gets closed technically, not just contractually.** This is the piece that goes beyond copying Ziidi Trader: since AfriSpine already has virtual-account infrastructure available through Fincra, use it to generate a **dedicated virtual collection account per order or per user**, pre-registered as belonging to the broker's settlement account rather than any AfriSpine-controlled account. The user's payment is then routed directly there by construction. This turns "AfriSpine is non-custodial" from a policy statement into a technical fact — the money is never capable of resting in AfriSpine's name, not even briefly, because the account it lands in was never AfriSpine's to begin with. Ask Zai to scope this specifically: can Fincra (or the broker's own bank) issue single-use or per-user virtual accounts tagged to the broker's settlement account, and can that be wired into the order flow so funding and order placement become the same atomic step?
3. **AfriSpine's own sub-ledger is still required** (as established in Section 6.1) — it reconciles continuously against the broker's records, exactly as Ziidi Trader must be doing today. This isn't optional infrastructure; it's the only reason the mirror is trustworthy.
4. **Legal role stays "introducer," formalized in writing** — same requirement as Section 6.1, now easier to point to precedent: Safaricom's relationship with Kestrel for Ziidi Trader is the model to ask Kestrel (or whichever broker) to replicate contractually with AfriSpine.

Unlike the currently-unnamed Kenya-side broker behind MyStocks, Kestrel is independently confirmable on NSE's own public list of trading participants — which immediately removes one layer of ambiguity if AfriSpine can establish they're the same entity, or build a direct relationship either way.

## 7. Two critical bugs already flagged — Priority 0, bank-grade bar

You've already told Zai about two specific defects. Restate them here as non-negotiable, and set the standard explicitly: this platform moves real money across borders and will soon process a landmark IPO — it needs to hold up to a **bank-grade** standard: no silent data loss, no lost credentials, full audit trails, and graceful (not silent) failure. "It usually works" is not the bar.

1. **"Login failed"** — a generic message like this is itself a bank-grade problem, not just the underlying bug: it collapses several very different failure modes (wrong credentials, locked account, expired/invalid session token, backend timeout, rate limiting, database unavailable) into one unhelpful string, which means neither the user nor your support team can tell what actually happened. Require Zai to:
   - Break this down into the actual distinct causes and confirm which one(s) are occurring — this should be answered with log evidence, not a guess.
   - Replace the single generic message with specific, safe error states (e.g. distinguishing "incorrect password" from "account temporarily locked" from "service temporarily unavailable — try again"), without leaking information that helps an attacker (never confirm/deny whether an email exists, for instance).
   - Add structured logging with a correlation ID per login attempt (success or failure) so any future login issue can be traced in minutes, not rediscovered by users reporting it.
   - Confirm whether this is affecting all users, a specific segment (device, browser, region, account age), or intermittently — that pattern usually points straight at the root cause.

2. **"Failed to fetch data" in the admin portal** — same principle: this is a caught error being shown verbatim instead of being diagnosed. This matters even more than a user-facing bug in some ways, because your own team relies on the admin portal to see what's actually happening on the platform — if it's unreliable, you're partially blind to your own operations, including the reconciliation work in Sections 5 and 6. Require Zai to:
   - Identify which admin features trigger it and why — API timeout, expired auth token, a backend endpoint erroring, CORS/network misconfiguration, or a downstream dependency (e.g. the same database-persistence issue below) being unavailable.
   - Confirm this doesn't share a root cause with the database persistence bug below — "fetch fails" and "writes don't persist" are exactly the kind of symptoms that trace back to one unstable data layer.
   - Add proper error surfacing in the admin portal specifically: a distinguishable message per failure type, plus retry logic where appropriate, since admins need to trust this tool completely.

3. **Database not persisting — Fincra test keys wiped repeatedly.** This is not a minor annoyance; it's a symptom of a structural problem, and it may well be connected to bugs 1 and 2 above — all three point at the same underlying question: is the data layer actually reliable under real conditions? This is directly relevant to Section 6, since the partner-broker integration for the Dangote flow depends on exactly this kind of persisted, encrypted credential storage working correctly under load. Ask Zai to:
   - Identify the actual root cause (is configuration being read from environment variables that reset on redeploy instead of a persistent encrypted store? Is there a failed write with no error surfaced? A cache being treated as the source of truth?)
   - Confirm API keys and other sensitive configuration are stored encrypted in persistent storage, not memory, local state, or anything that resets on deploy or restart.
   - Confirm this is fixed everywhere the same pattern could exist — payment provider keys, the partner broker integration credentials, any other third-party secrets — not just patched in the one place you noticed it.
   - Add a regression test so this class of bug can't silently return.

### 7.1 New bug — "Read full issue" fails with a Prisma sort-order error

This is the same *class* of bug as the login/signup issue, but not automatically fixed by that same migration — treat it as a separate, distinct fix, run separately, not folded into anything else. The error shows Prisma trying to order `DigestStory` records by a field called `sortOrder`, and the list of fields Prisma says are actually available on that model doesn't include it. That means the code assumes a `sortOrder` field exists on `DigestStory` that isn't actually present in the deployed schema.

**Before fixing, determine which of two situations this is** — the fix is different depending on the answer, so don't guess:
- **If `sortOrder` already exists in `schema.prisma`** but was never migrated to production (the same pattern as the login bug) → run the migration the same safe way as before: back up first, confirm the migration file exists, `prisma migrate deploy` to production, regenerate the client, redeploy.
- **If `sortOrder` was never actually added to `schema.prisma`** — i.e. the code was written assuming a field that was never designed into the schema at all → this needs a real field added: define `sortOrder` (an integer, used to control story display order within an issue) on the `DigestStory` model, generate the migration properly outside production, review the SQL, then deploy it the same safe way, backfilling existing stories with a sensible default order (e.g. by `publishedAt` or `createdAt`) so nothing breaks for content that already exists.

Either way: re-test "Read full issue" specifically after the fix, and — same lesson as last time — run the drift check across the *whole* `DigestIssue`/`DigestStory`/`DigestAdPlacement`/`DigestContributor` relation cluster while you're in there, not just the one field this error happened to surface.

### 7.2 African-relevant imagery on the digest — fix the source, not the runtime

Good news hiding in the error output you pasted: `DigestStory` already has `imageUrl`, `imageCredit`, and `imageSource` columns. That tells you the intended design was always to *store* a resolved image and its attribution per story, not fetch one live at page-load time. So the real bug isn't "we need to bolt on an image API" — it's "whatever is populating those existing columns right now isn't sourcing images that are actually relevant." Fix it at that level, and you avoid adding a new live dependency to the page-render path — which is exactly the kind of change that caused problems before.

**Where to make the fix**: in the content pipeline, at the point a digest story is authored or published — not in the frontend, and not on every page load. This keeps the user-facing app exactly as simple as it is today; only the publishing step changes.

**A free API worth using, chosen deliberately for low complications**: **Openverse** (openverse.org, run by WordPress/Automattic) is a strong fit here — it's a Creative Commons image search aggregator pulling from many sources including Wikimedia Commons, it's free with no API key required for basic use (removing yet another credential to keep persistent — worth avoiding another secret-management surface right now), and it returns proper license and attribution metadata per image, which maps directly onto the existing `imageCredit`/`imageSource` columns. **Pexels' API** is a solid alternative if a larger, more polished commercial-photo library is preferred — free, generous limits, no mandatory attribution, but does require an API key (one more credential to keep persistent, so worth weighing against Openverse's key-free option given recent history).

**The part that actually fixes "irrelevant Caucasian images" — query construction, not just API choice**: generic search terms like "finance," "investment," or "business" reliably bias toward default Western stock-photo results on almost any image API. The fix is to build the search query from data the story already carries — `countryFocus`, `exchangeFocus`, and `relatedTicker` all already exist on `DigestStory`. Use them: a story about Dangote should search something like "Lagos Nigeria refinery" or "Dangote Nigeria industry," a story about NSE/Safaricom should search "Nairobi Kenya stock exchange," and so on — specific and geographically anchored, not generic financial stock-photo terms.

**Given how visible and reputation-sensitive this is** (this is imagery representing the diaspora community the whole platform is built around), don't fully automate away human judgment here: add a lightweight review step in whatever admin/editor flow publishes a story, where the sourced image is shown before it goes live, so a genuinely wrong or low-quality match gets caught by a person rather than a user. A small curated fallback set of pre-approved, verified African-context images per country/category is worth having too, for whenever an automated search doesn't return a confident match — better than falling back to a generic default that risks the exact problem you're fixing now.

### 7.4 API health dashboard findings — MyStocks: wrong endpoints, not auth. Fincra: real auth rejection, not persistence.

The API Status page is genuinely good work — keep it, and from now on treat it (not a status update from Zai) as the source of truth for whether an integration actually works.

**MyStocks — "Configured" is not "Connected."** `/partner/profile`, `/markets/NSE/quotes`, `/markets/NGX/quotes`, `/opportunities`, and `/partner/earnings` all fail identically: HTTP 200 status, but the response body is HTML, not JSON ("mystocks API returned non-JSON response... Received an HTML error page instead of JSON"). This specific signature — success status code, wrong content type — is not what an authentication failure looks like; an auth failure returns 401/403 with a structured error body, exactly like Fincra does below. **200 + HTML almost always means the request landed on the wrong URL** — a marketing page, a redirect target, or a generic fallback page — not the real API.

The key evidence pointing at the fix: `GET /bonds` is the one endpoint that actually works, returning real data (6 bonds), and it took 1205ms — dramatically longer than the failing calls (13–222ms). The fast failures are bouncing off something immediately (consistent with hitting a cached page or wrong domain); the one real success took real backend processing time. **This means the MyStocks credential itself is very likely valid and the account works — the problem is that most of the endpoint paths/base URLs being called are wrong**, not that access is denied.

Action for Zai:
1. Pull MyStocks' current, actual API reference documentation directly — not from memory or a prior assumption — and check the exact base URL and path used for every failing endpoint, one at a time, against what's documented.
2. Compare each broken endpoint's URL construction against `/bonds`, the one known-working example. Check specifically whether `/bonds` uses a path prefix (e.g. an `/api/v1/partner/` style prefix, matching what MyStocks documents) that the broken endpoints are missing.
3. Add a content-type check before treating any API response as valid anywhere this pattern could recur: if a response isn't `application/json`, fail loudly and specifically rather than trying to parse HTML as data or silently returning wrong information.
4. Fix the "Configured" badge logic so it reflects real, verified connectivity (derived from these actual endpoint health checks) rather than "a credential value is present" — the current wording overstates how healthy this integration actually is, and that gap is exactly what caused the "MyStocks is connected" impression despite five of six endpoints failing.

**Fincra — this is a real 401, and persistence being fixed doesn't mean the key is right.** `GET /profile/business/me (Fincra)` returns a proper, well-formed JSON error: `{"message":"Unauthorized","request_id":"f3bc81081c6b88038b1a0c57e1491816"}`. That's Fincra's real server responding correctly — the request is reaching them fine, but the credential being sent is being rejected. This is a completely different problem from the earlier persistence bug, and fixing persistence doesn't fix this.

Action for Zai:
1. Confirm the exact key currently stored is the current, active key from Fincra's own dashboard — not an old, revoked, or placeholder value that happened to get saved once persistence started working.
2. Confirm environment matching: a sandbox/test key sent to a live endpoint (or the reverse) produces exactly this kind of 401 — check which environment the key belongs to versus which environment the endpoint being called actually is.
3. Test the same key directly against Fincra's documented endpoint outside the app (e.g. via curl or Postman) to isolate whether the key itself is invalid, or whether the app is sending it incorrectly (wrong header name, wrong format, e.g. missing a required `Bearer` prefix).

**The pattern worth naming explicitly, since it's the second time it's mattered this week**: "it's configured" and "the value persists" are not the same claim as "it works." From here on, nothing on this platform should be reported as fixed or connected until this health dashboard itself shows a real success, not a status report.
